The pronoun typically goes directly before the conjugated verb, though it can be attached to the end of infinitives or gerunds. [^1][^2][^3][^4]### Key Strategies for Mastering Reflexive Verbs:Understand the Formula: Structure sentences as [_Subject_] + [**Reflexive Pronoun**] + [***Conjugated Verb***].#### Memorize the Pronouns:|                   |        || ----------------- | ------ || **Yo**:                       | ***me***  || **Tú**:                       | ***te***  || **Él**/**Ella**/**Ud.**:      | ***se***  || **Nosotros**:                 | ***nos*** || **Vosotros**:                 | ***os***  || **Ellos**/**Ellas**/**Uds.**: | ***se***  |Identify Infinitive **-se**: Reflexive verbs in their infinitive form end in **-se** (e.g., lavarse, ducharse). Remove the **-se**, conjugate the verb normally, and add the correct pronoun.### Positioning:   - ***Before the Verb***: Ella se cepilla los dientes (She brushes her teeth).   - ***Attached to Infinitive***/***Gerund***: Voy a lavarme (I am going to wash myself) or Estoy lavándome.- **Common Usage Contexts**: Use them for daily routines (e.g., despertarse - to wake up), personal hygiene (e.g., bañarse - to bathe), and emotions (e.g., sentirse - to feel).- **Distinguish Reflexive** vs. **Non-Reflexive**: Despertar means "to wake someone else," while despertarse means "to wake oneself up". [^1][^2][^4][^5][^6][^7]### Common Reflexive Verbs to Practice:   - **Levantarse** (to get up)   - **Vestirse**   (to get dressed)   - **Sentarse**   (to sit down)   - **Llamarse**   (to be called/named)   - **Dormirse**   (to fall asleep) [^1][^2][^7][^8][^9]_______Mastering Spanish reflexive verbs involves using a *reflexive pronoun* (**me**, **te**, **se**, **nos**, **os**, **se**) that matches the subject to show the subject is performing the action on itself (e.g., Yo me lavo - I wash myself).
