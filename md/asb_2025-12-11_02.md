It would be difficult to imagine apps without any microinteractions implemented. They help convey brand image and personality to create experiences that are loved by users.As a motion ***designer***, you can create animations using *After Effects* and **Lottie**. Lottie is a small open-source, vector-based, interactive file format that can be modified at runtime. It converts vector animations into code and it can be used on different platforms like Web, Android or iOS apps. Thanks to this, developers don’t have to code your motion graphics anymore and you can be sure that the animations are pixel perfect.### <n>1</n> Install LottieFiles plugin###  To start your motion design journey, you need to download the Lottie plugin for After Effects from here:Once it’s been installed, you’ll find it at Window/Extensions/LottieFiles. You’ll be requested to log into your account the first time you use it.There are three tabs in the plugin window. The first tab allows you to see a preview of your animation. The second tab displays a list of all of the animations you’ve so far made. The third tab allows you to browse through hundreds of free animations created by other designers.LottieFiles plugin in Adobe After Effects##### <n>2</n> Create your animation###### Import from IllustratorAssume you want to animate a puppy running. To begin, use Adobe Illustrator to create a vector design of your character. Make sure each shape is on its own layer, and that all of your layers are appropriately named. Save your image as an Adobe Illustrator (.ai) file.Vector illustration prepared in Adobe IllustratorThen, switch to After Effects and import your .ai file as footage (File/Import/File). You should have all your layers visible in your composition. Select all the layers and convert your paths to shapes (Layer/Create/Create Shapes from Vector Layer). You can delete path layers since you will not need them anymore.## Animate shapesAny animation you make has to be Lottie compatible. Lottie does not support every After Effects functionality. A list of all features supported on Android, iOS, Windows, and other platforms can be found here:As a general rule, use keyframes and avoid applying any effects that rasterize your layers. You’ll be alright if you stay away from expressions, masks, mattes, and merged paths.In this example, I’m simply animating paths with a handful of keyframes to bring my dog artwork to life.Keyframe animation in After Effects## <n>3</n> Preview animationWhen you go to Window/Extensions/LottieFiles, you’ll be able to see all of the After Effects compositions you’ve created. You can preview how your animation will look on various backdrops. You can also enter a hex code for a specific colour.The render graph below depicts the impact of your animation on the CPU. You can always go back and simplify your animation based on this graph. You may also view your animation on your phone without having to upload it to LottieFiles. Every platform has its own player, and the players’ compatibility varies. If your animation works well on iOS, it’ll probably work well on other platforms as well.Preview Lottie animation in After Effects## <n>4</n> Export animationYou can save your animation as a JSON or Lottie using the LottieFiles plugin. These are the files you hand off to your developers. The main difference is that the .lottie file is typically smaller in size. To preview the interactions without After Effects you can upload these files here:When you switch to the Handoff tab, you can also edit the animation and export it to .gif. You can also scan the QR code to see the animation on the mobile app.## Lottie EditorYou don’t need After Effects to make adjustments to your animations. You may access Lottie Editor in your browser by clicking on edit animation in the handoff tab of the preview. The layers’ size, backdrop colour, playback speed, and layers’ colour can all be customized. You can also look through the layers and make changes on the fly. When you finish, you can either download or share a link to this revised animation with your developers or stakeholders.## Figma pluginUnfortunately, Figma doesn’t support Lottie yet but there is a way to preview your animation with an additional plugin:It converts your microinteraction into a gif which can be placed in a frame and played in Preview mode. It’s a workaround since you lose all of the advantages of having a vector image but this may suffice in some cases.Figma plugin converts Lottie to gif which can be used in Preview modeWhen you want to create high-fidelity prototypes, you might want to use ProtoPie instead.## ConclusionLottie is a library that can be used in apps to animate vector graphics. It allows developers to create animations with rich, dynamic content and deliver highly responsive, smooth and jitter-free motion. Lottie can be used for digital products to enhance the user experience.
