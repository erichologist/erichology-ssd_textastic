<p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">I love ampersands. And interest in this quirky character seems to be on the rise as of late. Case in point: Just last week, I purchased not one, but<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>
<em style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 13.104px; vertical-align: baseline; font-style: italic;">two</em>
<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>t-shirts adorned with nothing more than an ampersand. That’s telling, no?</p>

<p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">For much of last year, I had a little portion of a presentation dedicated to using<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>
<abbr style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 13.104px; vertical-align: baseline;">CSS</abbr>
<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>to serve up alternate, compelling ampersands. It’s also something I’ve done here on SimpleBits for quite awhile in our tagline. The simple little concept comes from one of Robert Bringhurst’s guidelines in his seminal typographic bible,<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>
<cite style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 13.104px; vertical-align: baseline; font-style: italic;"><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20120311064258/http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/0881792063/ref=nosim/simplebits-20" style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 13.104px; vertical-align: baseline; text-decoration: underline; outline: none; color: rgb(252, 107, 53);">The Elements of Typographic Style</a></cite>.</p>

<p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">Guideline 5.1.3 offers this little tidbit regarding ampersands:</p>
<blockquote style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 14.56px; vertical-align: baseline; quotes: none;">
   <p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">In heads and titles, use the best available ampersand.</p></blockquote>
<p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">Bringhurst explains that frequently<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>
<strong style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 13.104px; vertical-align: baseline; font-weight: bold;">the italic version of an ampersand is more decorative and interesting than its roman counterpart</strong>, and goes on to suggest:</p>
<blockquote style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 14.56px; vertical-align: baseline; quotes: none;">
   <p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">Since the ampersand is more often used in display work than in ordinary text, the more creative versions are often the more useful. There is rarely any reason not to borrow the italic ampersand for use with roman text.</p></blockquote>
<p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">So why not apply this to the web? We can use<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>
<abbr style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 13.104px; vertical-align: baseline;">CSS</abbr>
<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>to cleverly specify a list of our favorite ampersands, with the reader getting the best one available. We’ll first apply a class wrapped around the ampersand we’d like to beautify like so:</p>

<div style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em;border: 0px;padding:1.1em; background-image:repeating-linear-gradient(90deg, rgba(25,25,25,1) 0%, rgba(33,33,33,1) 75%, rgba(33,33,33,0) 100%);border-radius:7px;vertical-align: baseline;">
   <code class="language-css" style="font-family:monospace;color:#00FFEE;font-weight:600;padding: 0px;padding-bottom:.4em;">HTML</code>
  <br>
    <code style="font-size: 1.2em;vertical-align: baseline;line-height: 1.6;padding: 0.8em;padding-left: 0.65em;" class="language-css">&lt;span class="amp"&gt;&amp;amp;&lt;/span&gt;</code>
</div>
<p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">And we’ll build a list of cool italic ampersands that readers might have installed by default, while also specifying the italic version:</p>

<div style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em;border: 0px;padding:1.1em; background-image:repeating-linear-gradient(90deg, rgba(25,25,25,1) 0%, rgba(33,33,33,1) 75%, rgba(33,33,33,0) 100%);border-radius:7px;vertical-align: baseline;">
   <code class="language-html" style="font-family:monospace;color:yellow;font-weight:600;padding: 0px;padding-bottom:.7em;">CSS</code>
  <br>
    <code style="margin: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 1.2em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;padding: 0.8em;padding-left: 0.65em;" class="language-css">span.amp {
       <br><span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span><span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span><span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>font-family: Baskerville, Palatino, "Book Antiqua", serif;
       <br><span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span><span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span><span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>font-style: italic;
       <br><span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>}</code>
</div>
<p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">We can weight our list, putting our favorites near the front, understanding that the reader may get one or none, depending on which operating system they’re on, and if they have the font installed.</p>

<p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">Thanks to the excellent efforts of<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>
<a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20120311064258/http://meaganfisher.com/" style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 13.104px; vertical-align: baseline; text-decoration: underline; outline: none; color: rgb(252, 107, 53);">our newly-hired help</a>, here are some charts showing some of the more interesting italic ampersands available as<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>
<strong style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 13.104px; vertical-align: baseline; font-weight: bold;">default fonts</strong>
<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>on Mac and Windows. These’ll help get you started building your own<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>
<em style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 13.104px; vertical-align: baseline; font-style: italic;">awesompersand</em>
<span class="Apple-converted-space"> </span>list.</p>

<p style="margin: 0px 0px 1.6em; padding: 0px; border: 0px; font-size: 0.9em; vertical-align: baseline; line-height: 1.6;">These charts are by no means comprehensive, but were created from the default lists from each operating system. Pre-bundled or third-party applications may install other cool fonts that could be common enough to use as well. If you have a favorite that’s not listed, let us know in the comments.</p>
